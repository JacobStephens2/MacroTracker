## 1. Summary

Macro Tracker is being renamed to **Fareloch**.

The old name was not a name. It was the literal search term for the product
category, shared with at least four shipping competitors and one near-identical
GitHub repository. It was undefendable as a mark, invisible in search, silent
about the one thing that makes this app different, and inconsistent with every
other project in the portfolio.

Fareloch is a coined compound — *fare* (food, in the oldest English sense) plus
*-loch* (a bounded, self-contained body of water, and an established suffix in
this portfolio via Nightloch). It reads as **a contained record of your daily
food**, which is a more accurate description of what the app does than "Macro
Tracker" ever was.

`fareloch.app` is registered. This document records the reasoning so it does not
have to be relitigated.

## 2. Why the old name failed

Listed in descending order of long-term cost.

### 2.1 It was a category label, not a product name

"Macro tracker" is the phrase users type into a search bar to find this *class*
of software. Naming the app that is equivalent to naming a text editor "Text
Editor." It cannot accrue reputation, cannot be quoted, and cannot be
recommended unambiguously. "I use Macro Tracker" identifies nothing.

### 2.2 Direct, shipping collisions in both app stores

- **MacroTracker: Calorie Counter** — live on both the App Store and Google
  Play, published by Josef Landing. Feature set overlaps almost exactly: food
  logging, macro targets, barcode scanning, custom meals, progress charts.
- **MacroFactor – Macro Tracker** — the App Store listing title for the Stronger
  By Science app, the category leader at $11.99/mo.
- **MacrosFirst – Macro Tracker** — Google Play listing from MacrosFirst, Inc.
  (Santa Rosa, CA).
- **Macro Tracker – MacroTrack** — another App Store listing.
- **Macro Tracker** — a plain Android app circulating on APK distribution
  channels.

This was not a case of being generic in the abstract. It was being roughly the
fifth product with the same string, behind at least one funded competitor.

### 2.3 A repository-level collision already existed

`evanjh19/Macro-Tracker` on GitHub carries near-identical positioning:
"conveniently track macronutrients and weight changes." Same name, same
category, same platform.

### 2.4 "Macro" is polluted on this specific profile

On a GitHub profile that ships Rust and infrastructure tooling, "macro" reads as
`macro_rules!` before it reads as macronutrients. A GitHub search for "macro
tracker" returns `AaronErhardt/Tracker` — a Rust crate for tracking struct field
changes. Excel macros and keyboard macros compound the ambiguity. The word
actively works against comprehension here in a way it would not on a
fitness-only profile.

### 2.5 The trademark neighborhood is being actively claimed

**AI MACRO TRACKER** is a live USPTO registration (serial 98906450, registered
2025-09-08, class 042 — software services). This does not block use of a
descriptive phrase, and that is precisely the problem: the phrase is not ownable
by anyone, including me, while adjacent parties are staking out defensible
ground around it.

### 2.6 The name hid the actual differentiator

This app does not beat MacroFactor on algorithms and never will. It wins on
**ownership**: self-hosted, a SQLite file on my own server, CSV export, no
subscription, no data harvesting. Peers in that niche — SparkyFitness,
OpenNutriTracker, FoodYou, Waistline — all chose real names. "Macro Tracker"
communicated none of the thesis.

### 2.7 It was the last unnamed project in the portfolio

Chart35, Quadrille, Nightloch, Verelle, Inkvoke, Keeplore. Every other project
has an ownable name and, in most cases, a decision record like this one. Macro
Tracker was the outlier — a description wearing a name badge.

## 3. Why Fareloch

### 3.1 Meaning

**Fare** — food, in the oldest and most durable English sense: *bill of fare*,
*daily fare*, *simple fare*. It predates and outlasts the clinical vocabulary of
"macros," "intake," and "calories." It frames eating as something lived and
habitual rather than optimized, which matches an app used every day for every
meal, indefinitely.

**-loch** — a loch is a contained body of water: bounded, deep, and singular.
Applied to software it carries a custody connotation. Food records do not flow
out to someone else's cloud database; they sit in a bounded store I control.
The suffix signals enclosure and ownership without spelling it out in marketing
copy.

**Together** — *a contained record of your daily food*. A private ledger of what
you eat. Macros, calories, weight trend, recipes, and exports are all fare being
logged into a loch.

### 3.2 It extends a house convention

Nightloch already established `-loch` in this portfolio, with its own
NIGHTLOCH-NAME.md. A second `-loch` converts a one-off into a deliberate naming
system — the way `-Kit` works for Apple. A third would make it a signature.

### 3.3 It avoids an exhausted prefix

The entire `Macro-` compound space is picked over by live products: MacroLog,
MacroMate, Macrovia, Macrofolio, MacroVault, MacroKeep, Macrofare, MacroLoom,
MacroDeck. Any name in that family trades one collision for another. Fareloch
leaves the neighborhood entirely.

### 3.4 It avoids gym-culture framing

No "gains," no "fit," no "pal," no "tracker." The register is closer to a
kitchen journal than a cutting-cycle utility, which better fits a tool intended
for indefinite daily use.

### 3.5 Mechanics

- Pronunciation: **FAIR-lock**. Unambiguous, one reading.
- Two syllables, eight letters, survives being said aloud.
- No spelling trap, no numerals, no doubled letters.
- Kebab-case repo name is trivially `fareloch` — no hyphen needed.

requires a TESS search on the exact string plus phonetic equivalents
(Farloch, Pharloch, Fairloch, Farlock). For a self-hosted personal-scale
product the residual risk is negligible — and materially lower than the
undefendable-descriptive-phrase exposure being escaped.

## 6. Naming architecture

- **Brand:** Fareloch
- **App / store title:** **Fareloch — Macro & Calorie Log**
- **Short description:** Private food, macro, recipe, and weight tracking
- **Repository:** `fareloch`

  and calories. Meal logging, food search via Open Food Facts and USDA, barcode

  `barcode-scanner`, `recipe-manager`, `weight-tracker`, `express`, `nodejs`

  alias)
- **Systemd unit:** `macros-api` → `fareloch-api` (rename last)

The descriptive phrase survives in the subtitle, description, and topics — that
is where SEO lives. The title becomes ownable. This is the same split used for
the Inkvoke rename.

## 8. Visual direction

The loch is the organizing image, not the fork.

- **Palette:** deep water — slate blue, dark teal, near-black — with a single
  warm accent for the calorie ring.
- **Mark:** a bounded still-water shape, or a simple enclosed basin. Avoid the
  apple, the fork, the dumbbell, the flexing arm.
- **Dark mode is primary.** The app already honors `prefers-color-scheme`; dark
  should read as the intended default.
- **Continuity:** should feel like a sibling of Nightloch without being a copy —
  shared suffix, shared restraint, different accent.

## 9. Naming rules

1. Always one word, capital F: **Fareloch**. Never "FareLoch," "Fare Loch," or
   "fareLoch".
2. Lowercase `fareloch` in code, repo names, package names, hostnames, and
   systemd units.
3. The em-dash form **Fareloch — Macro & Calorie Log** is for store listings and
   the site title only. Body copy uses "Fareloch" alone.
4. Never write "the Fareloch app" in first-party copy. It is Fareloch.
5. "Macro tracker" remains valid as a *descriptor* in prose and metadata. It is
   never the name.

## 10. Migration checklist

Sequenced by risk. Domain is already acquired, so start at step 2.

1. ~~Register `fareloch.app` at Porkbun~~ — **done 2026-08-01.**
2. **Terraform / DNS** — add the zone, point `fareloch.app` at the Droplet,
   issue Let's Encrypt certs before any cutover.
3. **Rename the GitHub repo** `macro-tracker` → `fareloch`. Never delete —
   GitHub preserves the redirect. Update the description; leave topics intact.
4. **JWT cookie domain** — cookies scoped to `macros.stephens.page` will
   invalidate on a domain change. Either plan a forced re-login with a notice,
   or run a dual-domain cookie window during transition. This is the most likely
   source of silent user-visible breakage.
5. **`APP_URL`** — drives email verification and password-reset links through
   Mandrill. A stale value silently breaks account recovery. Update in the
   systemd unit and any `.env` before cutover, not after.
6. **Service worker** — `vite-plugin-pwa` will serve the cached old manifest,
   name, and icons to already-installed instances. Bump the SW version, handle
   `skipWaiting`, and update `manifest` name / short_name / icons.
7. **TWA** — `capacitor.config.ts` appId and the Digital Asset Links
   `assetlinks.json` are origin-bound. Changing hosts breaks URL-bar hiding
   until asset links are re-verified on `fareloch.app`. Do this before touching
   the Play listing.
8. **Cloudflare 301** — path-preserving redirect from `macros.stephens.page/*`
   → `fareloch.app/*`. Keep the old hostname alive indefinitely as an alias.
9. **In-repo strings** — README, DEPLOYMENT.md, DEVLOG.md, `deploy.sh`,
   `deploy-server.sh`, `capacitor.config.ts`, `package.json` name field,
   `index.html` title, manifest, email templates.
10. **Systemd unit** `macros-api` → `fareloch-api` — **last**, after everything
    else is verified. Update `deploy-server.sh` in the same commit.
11. **Play Store listing** — title and assets, only after asset links verify.
12. **Add this file** to the repo alongside DEVLOG.md, matching the Nightloch
    and Keeplore precedent.

## 11. Decision

**Rename Macro Tracker to Fareloch.** Domain acquired. Clearance clean. The old
name was a description; the new one is a name, it extends an existing house
convention, and it says something true about the product — that it keeps a
bounded, private record of what you eat.
